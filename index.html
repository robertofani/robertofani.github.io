Work in Progress

Labor Market Size and the Scope of Job Search

PDF|Online Appendix

Abstract

We study how local labor market size affects the cost of job displacement and the scope of job search. Using French matched employer–employee data and a matched difference-in-differences design, we estimate the causal effects of establishment closures and compare displacement losses across occupational markets within commuting zones. Workers displaced in larger markets experience smaller employment and earnings losses and return to occupations closer to those they left. A 10th-to-90th percentile difference in market size is associated with annual earnings losses smaller by 13 percentage points of pre-displacement earnings. Administrative job search records reveal a corresponding pattern in intended search: jobseekers in larger markets target occupations closer to their previous occupation and declare higher reservation wages. We develop a semi-directed search model in which workers choose search breadth, trading off access to vacancies against match quality. Larger markets relax this tradeoff, allowing more selective search alongside faster re-employment. In this environment, we show that equilibrium search is inefficiently broad even when the standard DMP efficiency condition holds.
